01 / Premise

Clustering is visible.
Its cause is not.

Events can occur close together because some places carry persistently higher risk, or because a recent event adds short-lived predictive information. Both processes can leave the same visual trace.

This thesis combines daily count models, Hawkes processes, spatial networks, conditional permutation tests and cross-city replication—without treating statistical dependence as proof of causation.

Interpretation

Prediction is not transmission.

Near-repeat dependence does not show that one incident caused another. Unmeasured local conditions can connect events too.

06 / About the research

Four questions
guide the work.

  1. 01

    How much short-term clustering remains after accounting for measured background risk?

  2. 02

    How sensitive is it to model design, event treatment, kernels and evaluation periods?

  3. 03

    Is recent activity in adjacent areas more informative than activity elsewhere in the city?

  4. 04

    At what spatial and temporal scales does violence cluster, and how does that vary by offence and city?
